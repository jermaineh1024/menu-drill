Floor thaw: bread in a pinch

Short on thawed dough? Frozen loaves can thaw at room temperature in about an hour and a half. It works. Overnight in the cooler is still better.

  • When: a busier day than planned, or anything else that leaves you short.
  • How: let the frozen dough sit in the bread rack at room temperature for an hour and a half, or until it's thawed all the way.
  • Check it often. If it looks dry, mist it with water.
  • Then proof and bake as usual.
  • What you give up: the dough misses its overnight aging, so the color, texture and flavor can come out slightly different. It's still very good bread.
  • The real fix: bake enough in the first place. Track your bread and tell the team, so tomorrow's number is right.

Rosemary parm bread

Bread with Parmesan and rosemary baked on top. Score, mist, cheese, rosemary, bake.

For each loaf

  • Parmesan: half an ounce. About a third of a 4 oz soufflé cup.
  • Rosemary: 1/16 of an ounce. About 2 small pinches.

How

  1. Proof the bread all the way, then score it the way you always do.
  2. Mist it with water. The water is what makes the toppings stick.
  3. Sprinkle the Parmesan across the loaf. Cover the ends.
  4. Rosemary on top of the cheese, so there's rosemary in every bite.
  5. Bake as normal. Make mini loaves the same way.

Good to know

  • Post the rosemary parm chart in the prep area so everyone can see how it's made.
  • Shredded, not grated. Shredded melts nicely.
  • Parmesan: 90-day shelf life, 4 five-pound bags to a case. Extra bags can go in the freezer. An open bag goes into a 4-quart Cambro (a clear food storage tub) in the walk-in.
  • Chopped rosemary: 1,095-day shelf life. That's three years.
  • Show it off in a rush: in front of the slicer, or a few loaves on top of the meat case.
  • Leftover loaves can be stored and used for hot subs the next day.
  • About 20% of your bread is usually rosemary parm. Track it on the production report so you never run out.

Gluten-free bread

One rule runs the whole thing: nothing that touched regular bread touches this sub. Clean first, fresh gloves, its own paper, its own knife.

The rolls

  • They come fully baked, each one wrapped, 12 to a case.
  • Into the freezer on delivery. Good for up to 6 months frozen.
  • To thaw: pull what you need onto the back counter. Never the bread rack. Leave each roll in its wrapper until it's served.
  • About 40 minutes to thaw, so pull them at 9:15 a.m. as part of opening.
  • Thawed and still wrapped: good for 5 to 7 days. Opened: wrap what's left in plastic right away and use it that day.

A cold sub

  1. Clean the slicer and the counters.
  2. Everyone who will touch the sub washes their hands and puts on fresh gloves.
  3. Tear a sheet of parchment paper. The sub is made on it, start to finish.
  4. Unwrap the roll. Cut it with the clean knife that is only for gluten-free. The lid stays on the paper.
  5. Slice it. The roll is a little smaller, but the meat and cheese portions are the same as a regular or a giant.
  6. Pass the paper, the sub and the knife to the sprinkler, who sprinkles it right on the paper.
  7. Wrap it on a fresh sheet, cutting it with the same gluten-free knife.
  8. Label it GF.
  • All the toppings are gluten-free. For mayo or cherry pepper relish, use the squeeze bottles in the meat case, not the spatulas.

A hot sub

  • Same rules. Clean the grill top, the counters and the tools. Wash hands, fresh gloves, its own parchment, the gluten-free knife.
  • No pocket. Cut the roll all the way through, like a cold sub.
  • Once the fillings are on the bottom half, cut regulars in half and giants in quarters.

Guests who order gluten-free watch every step. Smile, be professional, and do every step every time.
