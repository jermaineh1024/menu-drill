How a check works

Each station is trained in three kinds of shift. Introduction: the trainer shows you. Practice: you do it with them beside you. Confidence: they watch you work a real shift and tick a list.

Miss something and it isn't a fail. They talk it through, show it again, and you practice it. Everything ticked means the manager hears you're confident at that station.

The lists below are those confidence lists, in plain words. Tick the lines you already do without thinking. Your ticks stay on this phone only.

Sprinkler check

What the trainer watches for while you sprinkle.

  • I look up, smile, and talk with the guest and the team.
  • I ask "Mike's Way?"
  • The sub goes all the way down the board. Then it doesn't move.
  • Right amount and technique: onions, lettuce, tomatoes, the juice, the spices, extras.
  • I sweep the board before the juice.
  • Clubs always get their bacon and mayo.
  • Everything is spread evenly, ends covered.
  • Lids go on gently.
  • I pick subs up by the ends. No sliding.
  • My area stays clean and stocked.
  • I move with urgency and stay accurate.

After you pass, keep working on

  • 2 rows of juice for 2 oz, at least 8 times out of 10.
  • The Sprinkle Dance.
  • 3 subs at one time.
  • Running the sprinkling drills with a new person.

Wrapper check

What the trainer watches for while you wrap.

  • Eyes up, smiling, joining the banter.
  • Paper pulled to the right length, one sheet at a time.
  • Sub picked up by the ends and set in the center of the paper.
  • Cut properly: hand cupped, knife kept in a safe place.
  • Proper pullback, then the quarter turn.
  • Ends wrapped tight. No wings sticking out.
  • Hand-over-hand roll. No folds, thumbs stay down.
  • A neat, tight wrap, sub facing the right way.
  • Bag popped and creased properly.
  • I tell the cashier what's coming, and mark the number on the bag.
  • I move with urgency and stay accurate.

The extras a strict eye looks for

  • You ask "For here?" and never "To go?" It's more inviting.
  • You don't ask how many times to cut the sub. Use that moment to talk with the guest.
  • More than 2 items get bagged. A 12 lb. bag holds 3 to 4 subs, a 20 lb. bag 4 to 5. Subs first, then drinks, then chips and cookies.
  • You hand the bag to the guest. You don't slide it across the counter.
  • Digital orders are sealed in handled bags, hot subs on top of cold or packed apart, napkins in, and double-checked.

After you pass, keep working on

  • No wrapper lean.
  • Keeping orders organized, hot subs included.
  • Add-ons, and helping the cashier.
  • Running the wrapping drills with a new person.

Cashier check

What the trainer watches for at the register.

  • Eyes up, smiling, joining the banter.
  • I know the register's functions and parts.
  • I ring sales in correctly.
  • I ring in discounts and coupons.
  • I can handle refunds, voids and cancels.
  • Money habits: no gloves on money, hands washed.
  • I can activate and load gift cards.
  • I can use Shore Points.
  • I can use the Customer Facing Terminal, the screen the guest uses.
  • I know the money handling do's and don'ts.
  • I move with urgency and stay accurate.
  • I thank every guest and mean it.

Cashier training also covers register safety, and spends thirty minutes on the robbery section of the Safety First booklet. Ask your manager to walk you through it.

Grill check

What the trainer watches for at the grill.

  • I talk with guests and the team, and I'm smiling.
  • Bread cut and tempered properly.
  • Vegetables portioned properly.
  • Right order: the steak goes down last.
  • Steak salted after the flip.
  • Right chopping technique and count.
  • Tools held properly.
  • I can make beef, chicken and portabella subs the right way.
  • I know how the cheesesteaks differ.
  • Fillings spread evenly on the sub.
  • Every hot sub in the hot sub paper, wrapped loosely.
  • No more than three subs at a time.
  • Grill scraped between subs as needed.
  • I move with urgency and stay accurate.

The extras a strict eye looks for

  • You never start a sub on a dirty spot. Scrape first.
  • Tools by color: red handles for beef, yellow for chicken, black for mushrooms.
  • No banging the tools.
  • A regular portion of onions (1.25 oz) and peppers (0.75 oz) is on display by the grill, so anyone can check by eye.
  • The finished hot sub and its ticket go to the register together. If you bring it to a guest, find them. Don't shout their name.

After you pass, keep working on

  • Splitting the work when two people are on the grill.
  • Keeping up with tickets without overloading the grill.
  • Running the grill tools drill with a new person: 16 breaks, 4 x 4 x 4 x 4.

Slicer check

What the trainer watches for on the slicer.

  • I greet the guest, start the banter, and smile.
  • I tell the team the sub and who it's for.
  • Safe at every step: posture, loading and unloading, the guard.
  • Bread cut right, lids placed next to the sprinkling board.
  • Catch and flip. Built up, not out. Piled high.
  • Equal distribution, ends covered.
  • Cheese thickness: 5 slices of provolone = 2 oz.
  • Meat thickness: 3 slices of ham = 1.8 oz.
  • Meat case door shut every time.
  • Hot sub tickets written right.
  • Subs passed by the ends.
  • 2 to 3 subs at a time, and the line keeps moving.
  • Eyes up, 360° vision: I watch everything.
  • Slicer wiped often enough. I can clean it, sharpen it, oil it, take it apart and put it back.
  • I move with urgency and stay accurate.

The ham number shows up three ways in the training: 3 slices for 1.8 oz on this list, 5 slices for about 3 oz on the fundamentals sheet, and 8 slices for 4.75 oz in the video. All three are the same thickness, about 0.6 oz a slice.

The extras a strict eye looks for

  • 5 hot, wet towels ready, and the slicer wiped after every order.
  • White bread unless the guest says otherwise. You don't ask "White, wheat or rosemary?"
  • The scale is in place and working. Pens are uncapped and tickets pre-torn on top of the meat case.
  • You weigh subs through the rush, and digital, phone and catering orders regularly. Never take slices off a heavy sub.
  • The bread knife is sharp and kept next to the meat case, blade facing away.

After you pass, keep working on

  • Controlling the pace of the line.
  • Peripheral hearing.
  • Changing the play when it's needed.
  • Taking responsibility.
  • Head up, eye contact, every guest.
