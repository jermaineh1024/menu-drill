Closing faster

A fast close starts hours before the doors lock, and it never costs a guest their sub.

All evening

  • Wash dishes as you go. A few take no time. A pile costs someone 20 minutes at the end of the night.
  • Fill the chip racks and soda case early, then top them off.
  • Change out the pans in the sandwich unit, steam tables and toppings early, and restock before close.
  • An empty sauce bottle gets washed, rinsed and sanitized. Never just refilled.
  • After the case doors are clean, put plastic wrap on the handles so they don't smudge.

Later in the evening

  • Close half the meat case. Wrap the extra meats and cheeses. Keep out the ones you still need.
  • Sweep and mop the walk-in and freezer before the bread is racked, so the racks never have to be moved.
  • Two people rack the bread if you can. It's quicker and the dough doesn't start to defrost.
  • Pre-clean the grill with oil and a grill screen, while it's still on. Wear oven mitts. Don't use a grill brick yet: it leaves a residue you can't clear fast if someone orders a hot sub.
  • Clean the slicer one piece at a time. Take one piece to the sink, wash, rinse, sanitize, put it back. The slicer is never more than one piece from ready.
  • Clean the dining room, bathrooms and counter area. Don't mop them until the store is closed.

The rule over all of it

  • You serve guests right up to closing time. Never turn one away because something has been cleaned.
  • A hot sub on a pre-cleaned grill cooks fast, and a grill is easy to clean again after one or two subs.
  • If the slicer is apart, put it back together quickly and serve them with a smile.

A great close is a great open

Closing means resetting the store. Whatever tonight's team skips, tomorrow's opener has to make up before they can start.

Everything in its place

  • Grill: utensils washed and back at the grill shelf and steam tables.
  • Front counter: knives, bottles, shakers, the sprinkling board and the trough back in place.
  • Tea urns reset, spigots closed.

Product ready to grab and go

  • Onions peeled and washed.
  • Lettuce bopped and washed.
  • Tomatoes red, ripe, rinsed and ready.
  • All of it prepped the day before and waiting in the walk-in. Nothing slows an opening like having to prep these.
  • The extras (pickles, peppers) topped off, so the opener only has to drain and fluff them.

Every area set

Dining room, fountain counter, chip racks, soda case, bathrooms, grill area, front counter, kitchen and sinks.

If you're the opener

  • Something was missed? Say so. Tell the manager and the team. It isn't the end of the world, but they need to know.
  • Then do the same for the next shift. Before you leave, set them up.
  • A bad opening follows the store all day. A surprise catering order or a call-off on top of it turns a normal morning hectic.

Leading the close

You have the keys. Your job is to keep guests first, keep everyone moving through the checklist, and hand tomorrow a clean start.

  1. Ask the daytime shift leader how the day went. You learn if the after-lunch checklist is done, and if you need extra prep.
  2. Do any extra prep before the dinner rush is in full swing: bread, bacon, produce. You don't want to run out.
  3. Check for catering orders for tomorrow. Set up the boxes and do the extra prep. Write that prep on the Production Pro chart.
  4. Huddle. A quick one, to divide the closing checklist. Everyone knows who is doing what.
  5. Start the closing list early. Before the dinner rush is the time for the more thorough cleaning.
  6. Initial each task as it's finished.
  7. Finish the Production Pro chart: fill in waste and left. That gives you usage.

How a good lead acts

  • The customer comes first. When one walks in, everyone stops and serves them. That's a must.
  • Offer help. "Can I help you with that?" Never hesitate to help with someone else's task.
  • Stay active when it's slow. When the rush dies down, rally the team around the checklist.
  • If you start a task, finish the task.
  • Something clean gets dirty again? No big deal. Wipe it. Keeping things clean and stocked all night is what saves time.
