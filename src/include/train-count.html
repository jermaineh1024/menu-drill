Inventory: the math

Inventory answers one question: how much money is all the product in the store worth? From that you get what you used, and food cost.

  • Beginning inventory is the dollar value of the food and paper in the store when the period starts.
  • Purchases are everything delivered during the period: Sysco, Pepsi, produce.
  • Beginning + purchases = working inventory. Everything that was in the store at some point.
  • Closing inventory is the count at the end. It becomes next period's beginning.
  • Working minus closing = cost of goods used. It's shortened to COGU. COGS, cost of goods sold, means the same thing.
  • Divide by sales to get a percent. A percent can be compared with last week and with the store's goal.

The video's example

$5,000 to start + $7,000 in purchases = $12,000. Minus $5,500 at the close = $6,500 used. On sales of $23,215 that's 28%.

When

  • Weekly is best. The period runs Monday through Sunday. A food cost problem gets caught and fixed in days, not weeks.
  • Monthly counts fall on the last Sunday of the month or the last day of the month, depending on how the books are kept.

Before you count

A good count is set up before it starts: an organized store, the right hour, a partner, and the right tools.

  • When: Sunday at close, or Monday before open. The same time every week, and when you're fresh. Not after a long day.
  • Allow at least 1 hour to count. Then separate time to enter it, and time to review it.
  • Sunday evening: stock the working areas early (meat case, sandwich unit, displays). Count the storage areas through the evening. Count the working areas after the store closes.
  • Monday morning: working areas first, then storage. Finish before the store opens. If you can't, storage can still be counted after open.
  • Team up. One counts and weighs, one writes. Then switch. It's the best way to teach someone inventory.
  • Keep the store organized all week. Every delivery goes in its proper place. The count is faster and more accurate.

Bring

  • The count sheet, printed from CrunchTime, on a clipboard.
  • A pencil with an eraser.
  • Extra paper and a calculator.
  • A scale that works and reads right.

The count sheet

One row for each product, grouped by where it's stored. You write how much you have, in whichever unit fits what's in front of you.

  • Seq means sequence. The video leaves the details for a later CrunchTime lesson.
  • Inventory unit is how the item is counted. Bacon is counted by the pound. Other items go by the case, box, sleeve, each, bag or gallon.
  • Qty is where you write how much you have.
  • Alt units are other ways to count the same item. For bacon: case, sheet and slice. So an open case can be 5 pounds, plus 4 full cases, 6 sheets and 12 slices. You count what's there. You don't have to estimate.
  • CrunchTime does the dollars. It multiplies your counts by the current price.
  • Some things do need an estimate, like an open bag-in-the-box of soda. Use sound reasoning.
  • The sheet goes by storage location. Provolone is in the meat case, the walk-in and the sandwich unit. Count everything in one place, then move to the next. No running back and forth.
  • Double-check, above all the decimal points. 0.4 and 4 are very different. A slip like that throws off the whole food cost.

The most common causes of inventory mistakes are carelessness and not paying attention to detail.

Inventory in CrunchTime

Check the invoices, print the sheet, count, type it in, review, post. Then pull the report.

1. Check the purchase journal

  • Purchasing, then Reports, then Purchase Journal. Set the week's dates. Group by supplier. Retrieve.
  • Compare it with your paper invoices. The dates should match, and each order should show a reconciled date.
  • No reconciled date means it hasn't been reconciled. Do that first.
  • An invoice not on the list is usually Pepsi or produce. Place and reconcile it.

2. Print the count sheet

  • Create / Review Inventories. Check the post date. Count Sheet.
  • Filter icon, User-Defined Category. All is all food and paper. Consolidated is breads, meats and cheeses. Weekly Count is all the food and a few paper items. Pick Weekly Count. Apply.
  • Printer icon, Export, print.

3. Count, then enter

  • Create / Review Inventory, post date, Data Entry. Filter to Weekly Count again.
  • Type each count under its proper inventory unit.
  • If any products were left blank, By Exception fills them with an ideal amount, figured from what you started with, ordered and sold.
  • Save and Review. Check the physical column. Wrong number? Edit, fix it, Save and Review again.
  • Save and Close.

4. Post it, then read it

  • Administration, Post Inventory. The summary shows the period's purchases, what was consumed and the ending inventory. If it looks right, Post.
  • Inventory, Reports, Actual Theoretical Cost. Set the dates. Retrieve. Open a category, like meats, to see each product's variance.
