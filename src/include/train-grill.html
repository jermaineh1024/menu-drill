Grill basics and hot sub bread
  • Fast, calm, organized. Keep the tickets in the order they came in, and don't start a hot sub until you can give it your attention.
  • Scrape the grill as often as you can. A clean grill cooks quicker.

The bread is a pocket

  • Giants: run the knife across the front of the loaf, angled into the bread. Don't slice through the ends, and don't cut all the way through.
  • Regulars: cut the loaf in half first, then make the same cut in each half.
  • Pre-cut before the rush. It saves time while you're grilling.

Temper it: warm, not toasted

  • Open and close the pre-cut loaf, so the heat can reach the middle without toasting it.
  • Bottom side down on the grill, and turn it often so it warms evenly.
  • Leave it on as long as you can. Take it off when it needs its sauce or toppings.

Chicken cheesesteaks: 16, 42, 55

The same build as the beef, with chicken in place of steak.

  • The chicken goes on frozen, so it usually takes a little longer than the thawed beef.
  • Cook it all the way through, and don't dry it out. Move with urgency so it isn't overcooked.
  • Yellow tools: yellow-handled tongs, yellow spatula, yellow dough cutter. Avoid touching the raw meat.
  • Same portions as the 17: 15 inches of paper, 3 slices of cheese, 1.25 oz of onions, 0.75 oz of peppers on top of them.
  • Same breaks: flip, break 4, bring it together, break 4, onions and peppers on top, 8 more. Cheese on top with a few slits. Onto the bread, loose bits scraped on.
  • #42 Chipotle Chicken: 3 lines of chipotle mayo on the inside of the lid, like the 43.
  • #55 Big Kahuna Chicken: 4 slices of cheese, and 0.75 oz of mushrooms and 4 to 5 jalapeños go down with the onions and peppers.
  • Giants: double the portions, 21 inches of paper, cooked as two regular portions.

The video salts the steak right after the flip. For the chicken it only says to flip it.

Wrapping hot subs
  • Every grilled sub goes in the hot sub wrap. It holds the heat and lets the sub breathe, so it doesn't go soggy.
  • 15 inches for a regular, 21 for a giant.
  • Giants get cut in half. Regulars don't get cut. That's different from a cold sub.
  • Stand the sub up on its side, so everything stays in place.
  • Wrap it loosely, with the same steps as a cold sub. Never squeeze a hot sub.

Bacon
  • Before you start: grill preheated to 350°F, a 2.5-inch half pan with a grate, the bacon thawed, the steam table preheated.
  • About 4 sheets of bacon on the grill top.
  • Leave it alone. Flipped too early or too often, it sticks and shrivels. It cooks itself loose from the grill, and by then it's about 75% done.
  • Flip several pieces at a time with a spatula, keeping them straight, and cook the last 25% to golden brown.
  • Off the grill several pieces at a time, into the pan, and the pan goes into the steam table.
  • End of the night: leftover bacon can be wrapped and reheated the next day. FIFO.

Steaks: thaw the beef, keep the chicken frozen
  • Both arrive frozen and are stored in the freezer at 0°F.
  • Raw meat: if you touch it, wash your hands and change your gloves before you touch any other food.
  • Beef is panned between 2 and 4 p.m. the day before. The Production Pro chart and the register's production report tell you how much.
  • A 6-inch deep half pan with a drain grate. Stand the frozen steaks up, closed side of the paper facing out, and put the pan in the walk-in.
  • Ready at about 40°F: holding its shape, some ice crystals, still slightly frozen. An overthawed steak goes soft, cooks too fast and can overcook.
  • Chicken stays frozen. The grill person pulls it from the freezer as needed. If the store is busy enough, 4 to 6 frozen chicken steaks at a time can sit in the sandwich unit.
  • Chicken left in the sandwich unit at close is thrown out. It can't be refrozen.
