The day by the clock

A store day has six parts. Each part has its own job, and each one sets up the next.

The times are the training's examples. Your store's hours and rushes may sit a little earlier or later.

About 2 hours before the doors Open
Everything on. Bread proofed and baked in three waves. Cookies. Meatballs and sauce. Onions, lettuce and tomatoes sliced. Bacon. Slicer sharpened, oiled, cleaned, thickness set. Gluten-free rolls out at 9:15. Meat case and tea set. Who: the opener, then a second person. Tool: the opening checklist and the Production Pro chart.
Usually 11 to 2 Lunch rush
No prep. Best people in their best spots. The slicer runs the line, three subs at a time at most. Every sub is called down the line. Slicer wiped after each order. A guest walks in, everyone comes forward.
After lunch Mid-day
The mid-day checklist: temperatures logged, sanitizer changed, everything restocked, a full slicer clean. Bread, bacon and cookies topped up if needed. Yesterday's bread and onions leave the grill at 2. Tomorrow's beef is panned between 2 and 4. Tomorrow's lettuce, onions and tomatoes are prepped. The closing lead asks the day lead how the day went.
Usually 5 to 7 Dinner rush
Same as lunch. Before it starts: any extra prep is done, and the closers have split the closing checklist in a quick huddle.
Evening Close down
Dishes as you go. Restock. Half the meat case wrapped. Walk-in mopped, then bread racked after 7. Grill pre-cleaned with oil and a screen. Slicer cleaned one piece at a time. Guests are served right up to closing time. Never fewer than 2 people in the store.
After the doors lock Reset
Dining room, bathrooms and counter area mopped. Everything back in its place so the opener can grab and go. Production Pro chart finished: waste and left filled in.

The mid-day checklist

Between the rushes the store gets put back to full: temperatures logged, sanitizer fresh, everything restocked, tomorrow's prep confirmed.

This follows a mid-day checklist as one store's tablet shows it. Yours may differ a little, and the tablet is the one that counts. Tick these to practice. Your ticks stay on this phone only.

2. Sanitizer

  • Test strip for the sink reads 200 to 500 ppm. Photo taken.
  • Test strip for the bucket reads 200 to 500 ppm. Photo taken.
  • Red sanitizer buckets replaced with fresh sanitizer (every 4 hours).
  • Wash and sanitizer sinks changed.
  • Dishes washed, rinsed, sanitized.

3. Where guests are

  • Tables and chairs cleaned.
  • Dining room floor swept and mopped as needed.
  • Chip racks filled, rotated and dusted.
  • Drink coolers filled and rotated, oldest bottles in front.
  • Soda station checked and stocked. Cups restocked.
  • Syrup boxes and CO2 checked.
  • Napkins restocked, dispensers cleaned.
  • Every trash can checked and emptied.
  • Glass cleaned: sneeze guard, front door, meat case, soda case.
  • Bathrooms cleaned, sanitized and swept.

4. The line and the grill

  • Bread checked, and more baked if needed.
  • Slicer fully cleaned, and written on the slicer cleaning form.
  • Front and back line toppings cleaned and restocked to full.
  • Hot sub area cleaned and restocked.
  • Grill tools (tongs, spatulas) washed and rotated.
  • Front line tools (knives, spatulas) washed and rotated.
  • Ice bath on the digital line changed, if your store has one.
  • Vinegar and oil bottles cleaned and filled.
  • Meat case restocked.
  • Bacon checked, and more cooked if needed.
  • Cookies checked, and more baked if needed.
  • The scale cleaned in detail.
  • Mini cookie dough cut and prepped.
  • Production chart updated for everything above.
  • Front line checked one last time: fully stocked.

The tablet tags the first restock line and this last one "9:59." The training sheets print the same tag next to "clean and restock when there is a break in the action."

5. Tomorrow

  • Lettuce and onions prepped for tomorrow.
  • Tomatoes sorted and washed.
  • Tuna and chicken salad checked.
  • Freezer pulls done: Philly steak and bacon.
  • Kitchen swept and mopped. Freezer floor swept.

The store's list also has cash tasks and ends with the manager's initials. Your manager will show you those in person.

Training someone new

Show it, do it beside them, then watch them do it. Always say why. That's the whole method.

  • Introduction shift: you demonstrate at normal speed, then slowly, explaining the why of each move.
  • Practice shifts: they do it, first away from guests, then side by side with you on the line for 1 to 2 hours. Coach politely. Step in when needed.
  • Confidence shift: you watch them work and tick the list for that station. Anything missed: talk it through, show it, let them practice, and tell the manager.
  • As they get the hang of it, push the speed.

Station by station

Sprinkling
Onions, lettuce and tomatoes on the prep table, 30 minutes (10 on each). The juice into the sprinkle pan with water, 20 minutes: 2 rows should weigh 2 to 2.2 oz in the giant pan, 1 to 1.1 oz in the regular. Oregano with cornmeal into clean bread forms, 10 minutes. Then the front line.
Wrapping
Show the wrap, 30 minutes. Practice bread is 2 regular loaves stacked, smashed and wrapped in plastic. Then a Round Robin, 15 minutes: they wrap and bag one, hand it to you, you unwrap it and slide it back. Then the front line.
Cashier
The register side by side, 30 minutes: safety, the screens, a card, cash and gift card sale, counting change back, Shore Points, voids, cancels, refunds. Then 30 minutes on the robbery section of the Safety First booklet. Then the front line.
Hot subs
The tools, 10 minutes: onions on the grill, broken 16 times in a 4 x 4 x 4 x 4 pattern. Tickets, 5 minutes: slicer to grill to cashier. The charts, 5 minutes. Then a regular 17, 16 and 26 outside the rush, 30 minutes. Then the grill in a rush, with you beside them.
Slicing
Safety, the wipe-down, the full clean and cutting bread, 30 minutes. Posture and movements without slicing, 30 minutes. Thickness, coverage and weight, 1 hour: 5 slices of provolone for 2 to 2.2 oz, 5 of ham for 3 to 3.2 oz. Building a sub layer by layer on the scale, 30 minutes. First real subs come from digital or catering orders. Then the front line, with you watching everything.

Beyond the station, keep coaching

  • Keeping their area stocked and clean.
  • Checking the dining room.
  • Helping teammates.
  • Coming to the counter every time a guest walks in.
  • Finishing the checklist.
