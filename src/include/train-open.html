An opening in 2 hours

An opening fits in 2 hours when jobs overlap. Bread proofs while you slice. Bacon cooks while you slice tomatoes. Move with urgency and tick the checklist.

  1. Turn on all the equipment, so everything is at temperature when you need it.
  2. Music on, low enough to hear the phone and online orders. The TV stays off until right before open.
  3. Wash your hands and put on gloves. Wash often, all morning.
  4. Stretch all the bread. Fill and set up the sinks while you do.
  5. Cookies in. By the time the bread is stretched, the oven is hot.
  6. Check the bread count: what was racked, what was left over. Fill in the Production Pro chart as you go.
  7. Go time. Meatballs and sauce on the grill.
  8. Slice the onions and lettuce. Bring product, pans and lids to the slicer first. Check the chart for how much. Two at a time, one in front of the other. Cut in the lettuce left from yesterday.
  9. Second person arrives. See where you are and tell them what's needed. The register till and cash box get set up now.
  10. Check the meatballs and sauce, and start the bacon. After raw bacon: new gloves, washed hands.
  11. Slice the tomatoes, checking the bacon and the bread as you go.
  12. Set up the meat case and the tea urns. Clean the grill top.
  13. The checklist: confirm nothing was missed, and hand out the 10 to 10:30 tasks.

What makes it work

  • Layering. Start the job that runs by itself (bread, bacon, cookies), then do the hands-on one.
  • Check the bread often the whole time you're slicing.
  • Clean as you go.
  • Slicing on the front counter? Lean a large Cambro lid against the sneeze guard in front of the slicer. Cleanup is easier.
  • Tick the opening checklist as you finish each task, even if you've opened hundreds of times.

The video also says "remember floor time" when the bread comes out. It doesn't say how long. Ask your trainer how long your store rests the dough before it goes in the proofer.

Sharpen and oil the slicer

Every morning, as part of opening: sharpen for 10 seconds, hone for a second and a half, clean off the metal dust, then a few drops of oil.

Why: a sharp, oiled slicer gives the best slice, doesn't shred the meat, and keeps food cost in line.

You need

  • A clean slicer.
  • The sharpener, with clean, dry stones.
  • The slicer's machine oil, or a food-grade lubricating oil.
  • Cut-resistant metal mesh gloves and eye protection. Both are required.

Sharpen

  1. Put on the metal mesh gloves.
  2. Slicer off. Attach the sharpener, set to 0, and tighten its small knob. The blade is opened all the way.
  3. Put on eye protection.
  4. Switch the slicer on. Turn the sharpener's dial to the first setting for 10 seconds.
  5. Dial back to 0. Slicer off. Wait until the blade stops turning.
  6. Switch on again. Second setting for 1.5 seconds. This hones the edge.
  7. Slicer off. Wait for the blade to stop. Take the sharpener off and close the blade right away.
  8. Brush the stones quickly.
  9. Unplug the slicer and do a complete clean. Sharpening leaves metal dust.

Oil

  • A few drops on the shaft underneath. Slide the pusher deck back and forth to spread it.
  • A few drops on the shaft the guard glides on. Work the guard up and down a few times.
  • It doesn't take much.

Looking after the sharpener

  • Dirty stones stop working. The grinding stone should feel slightly rough.
  • It can be washed and sanitized at the 3-compartment sink. Use its brush, or a toothbrush like it, on the stones.
  • Let the stones dry before the next use. Wet stones can't sharpen.

Slicers come with one of two kinds of sharpener, and they attach in different ways. Have a trained person show you yours on the machine. Don't learn that part from a page.
