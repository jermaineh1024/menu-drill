CrunchTime in one minute

CrunchTime is the software that tracks the store's food, orders, counts and labor. In the store you use the part called NetChef.

  • Two parts. Enterprise Manager is used at the corporate offices. It holds the company's setup: every product, recipe, vendor and store. NetChef is used in the restaurant: vendor orders, inventories, sales forecasts, the schedule.
  • NetChef talks to the register. It pulls sales, checks and time punches from the POS, which is the register system.
  • What it gives you: orders placed and received in one place, so mistakes are easy to spot. A dashboard of your KPIs (key performance indicators, the few numbers that show how the store is doing). What food cost was, what it should have been, and where to look. Reports to compare weeks. A sales forecast for the week ahead. A task list of what's due.
  • Your part: enter inventories, orders and deliveries accurately, consistently and on time. Everything it tells you is only as good as what goes in.
  • Keep the CrunchTime reference sheet handy. It walks you through each task.

The CrunchTime video says its users routinely lower food cost by 2 to 5% of sales. In a store that does $1 million a year, that's $20,000 to $50,000.

Ordering: the goal and the order guide

Order enough to never run out, and no more than that. The target is enough in the store to last one full business day past your next delivery.

  • Who you order from: Sysco, Pepsi, and in some areas a local produce supplier.
  • Print the order guide from CrunchTime and put it on a clipboard. Walk the store with it.
  • Down the left side, products are grouped by category: all the breads under Food, Bread. All the cheeses under Food, Cheese. Read each product name carefully.
  • Vendor unit is how the product is packed. Red wine vinegar says 4/1 gal: 4 one-gallon bottles in a case. You count and write everything in vendor units.
  • The slash columns are where you work out the order. The next card shows how.
  • Your printed guide may also show par levels (the least and the most to keep on hand), the quantity available, and a 4-week average.

Before you decide

  • Are sales trending up or down? Weather, catering orders, holidays, the season and local events all change how much you need.
  • Do it at the same time each week. It becomes a habit, and stock levels stay steady.
  • Give it time. No distractions. Double-check your work.
  • Don't be the only one who can order. Hand it to someone on the team, then review their order with them.

The slash box math

Each product gets a box split by a slash. Top half: what you have. Bottom half: what you're ordering. Both are in cases, written as decimals.

  1. Write the date at the top of the slash column, on every page.
  2. Count what's on the shelf and turn it into cases. A case of vinegar is 4 bottles, so 2 bottles is 0.5. Write it in the top half.
  3. Decide the order and write it in the bottom half. Half a case won't last, so you order 1.
  4. Next order, count again. 1 bottle left is 0.25.
  5. Work out what you used. You had 0.5 and ordered 1, so 1.5 was in the store. 1.5 minus 0.25 left means 1.25 cases used.
  6. Order with a cushion. One case would bring you to 1.25, exactly what you used. A busy week or a broken bottle and you're out. So you order 2.

Comparing each order with the one before is what makes your ordering precise.

Placing and receiving an order

Placing an order is a short string of clicks. Receiving it means matching the paper invoice to the screen until "over/short" reads 0.

Print the order guide

  1. Quick Links: Create Vendor Order.
  2. Choose the vendor. Put in the expected delivery date. Continue.
  3. Order Guide, top right. Then Export, and print it.

Place the order

  1. Create Vendor Order. Vendor, expected delivery date, Continue.
  2. Type the quantity for each product, from your filled-in guide.
  3. Add Selected Products, top right.
  4. Review the list. Something missing? Retrieve, search for it, add its quantity, and Add Selected Products again.
  5. Prepare Order. Check it one more time. Submit.

Receive it (reconcile)

  • Sysco reconciles on its own most of the time. Pepsi and produce you do by hand.
  • Open Vendor Order Receive. From the paper invoice, type the invoice number and the invoice total, then the actual delivery date and the invoice date. Continue.
  • On the reconcile screen, make it full size with the icon at the top right. Check the two dates again.
  • Match the quantities to the paper invoice. Match the prices to the paper invoice.
  • If everything matches, over/short reads 0. Click Reconcile.
