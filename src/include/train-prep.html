Prep: the ground rules and how much
  • Everything is prepped fresh, every day.
  • Food safety always: washed hands, gloves, rinsed produce, temperatures.
  • Prep happens outside the rushes. Lunch is usually 11 a.m. to 2 p.m. Dinner is usually 5 to 7 p.m.
  • Customers first, prep second. Any time a guest is in the store, stop and help the team serve them.

Prepping the right amount saves time and cuts waste. Check with the manager for how much.

Onions: peel today, slice tomorrow

Peeling, the day before

  1. Cut off the root and the stem with a sanitized knife. On the root side, go just deep enough to take out the solid core. Cutting away more is waste.
  2. Slit about the first two layers along the side, and peel the skin away. Cut out any bad spots.
  3. Don't cut deep. A deep cut gives strings, not rings, when the onion is sliced, and gives germs a place to hide.
  4. Rinse under cold water at the prep sink, most of all where you cut.
  5. Into a food storage container and into the walk-in until tomorrow. These are the onions the opener slices.
  6. Wash and sanitize the work area.

Slicing

Never go by the dial. Measure against a tomato slice every time, and check again as you go.

  • Set up: slicer clean and fully assembled, a large bin for fluffing, third pans.
  • Check the thickness: slice 2 slices of onion, stack them, and hold them against a center slice of tomato.
  • Two onions at a time, similar in size, one in front of the other, either end down, with the pusher deck all the way back. Guard on top, right hand on the guard.
  • Push only the distance of the onions. If one shifts, bring the guard all the way back and set it again.
  • Slice all the way down. After each pair, move them to the bin and fluff: break them apart into rings without breaking the rings.
  • Into third pans. Lid or plastic wrap, date it, refrigerate. FIFO.

Lettuce: bop today, slice tomorrow

Bopping, the day before

  1. Bop the head core side down on a sanitized solid surface to loosen the core. Then twist and pull the core out. Only the core.
  2. Not too hard, not too soft. Too hard and the head goes loose and breaks apart. Too soft and the whole core won't come out.
  3. Pull off unusable outer leaves, as few as you can.
  4. Rinse the outside under cold water at the prep sink, core side down. Don't rinse inside the core.
  5. Core side down in a Cambro container, lid resting on top, not sealed. A little airflow helps the lettuce dry. Walk-in overnight.
  6. Wash and sanitize the work area.

Slicing

  • Lettuce is sliced after onions, so wipe the slicer down well first. Grit mat and green cutting board in place.
  • Thickness: two thirds of a center tomato slice. Set a tomato slice on the adjustable deck by the blade and open to slightly less. Never by the dial.
  • Two heads at a time, core side down. Don't reach in with your left hand. Guard on top, right hand on the guard.
  • Push only the distance of the lettuce, and don't push down. If one head is bigger, put it in front and slice it down to the size of the other, then slice through both.
  • Break it by hand on the green board: 4 times, 2 per head, with a ripping or tearing motion. Never a knife or a dough cutter. Cutting tools are too harsh on lettuce.
  • Lid or plastic wrap on the full pan, and date it.

Tomatoes: sort, rinse, slice

Sorting

  • Ripe takes both: a deep red color, and it gives a little when you squeeze it.
  • Only one of the two (red but hard, or soft but pale): the unripe case, for another day.
  • Going very soft with borderline color: the ripe case. Another day and it might go bad, so salvage what you can.
  • Lid on the unripe case, mark it P for pink, and date it.

Rinsing, the day before

  • Ripe tomatoes only, from the walk-in. Pull the stems.
  • Rinse each one under cold water at the vegetable sink.
  • Into a clean bin, covered, and back in the walk-in until tomorrow.
  • Never slice a tomato straight out of the box.

Slicing

  • Have ready: the tomatoes, enough half pans, and a clean, sanitized tomato slicer.
  • Stem side down on the slicer. Hands away from the blades.
  • One hand at the far end, where the tomato comes out, to stop it. The other on the pusher handle: push or tap it through evenly.
  • If it sticks, take it out. Forcing it bends and dulls the blades.
  • One tomato gives 8 to 9 usable slices, about a loaf and a half.

Celery and tuna

Celery

Celery carries a lot of foodborne pathogens, so the wash is the point.

  1. Chef's knife: cut the first ½ inch off the tops of all the stalks. That part is dry, discolored and dirty.
  2. Cut the bunch in half at the core, into two split bunches. Pull off all the leaves.
  3. Rinse from the core outward. Work the water between the stalks in a downward motion, so dirt washes away from the core. At least 30 seconds a split, 1 minute for the bunch.
  4. Slice at 3/16 inch, the same as a tomato. One split at a time, guard on.
  5. Stop with 2 inches left on each split. Embedded dirt gets thrown out with it, and the pieces come out more even.
  6. Tare a food prep pan on the scale and fill it to 30 oz. That's one batch of tuna.

Tip from the video: slice celery in the morning along with the onions and lettuce.

Tuna, one batch

  1. Get everything together first, so you can mix quickly.
  2. Open one pouch into a full-size Cambro bin and scrape the pouch clean.
  3. Break it down: a golf-ball-size handful between your palms, rubbed until it's fine. No chunks. One pouch at a time. The albacore is harder to break down than the light.
  4. Mix the two tunas together.
  5. Black pepper, sprinkled evenly over the top, mixed all the way in.
  6. Celery, mixed all the way in.
  7. Mayo, mixed all the way in. Every time, check the corners, the edges and the middle.
  8. Spatula it into 6-inch deep third pans with lids. Date every pan. Into the walk-in.
  9. Let it rest until the next day. It sets up and gets easier to spread.
  10. Stir it before serving. It separates as it sits.

The Production Pro chart

Prep today for what you need today. The chart shows what the store really uses, and in the video's words, "food cost is food used."

  • Count smarter: onions each, lettuce by the pan, tomatoes by the row, bacon by the sheet. One row of 7 tomatoes covers 10.5 loaves.
  • Check the subs against the bread. One pan of lettuce is 4 heads, enough for 20 loaves. If the store used 140 loaves, that should be 7 pans. If 10 went out, 3 pans are going somewhere: too much on each sub, or too much wasted in the trough.
  • Watch what's left. Two pans of lettuce left over every night means it's over-prepped, and day-old lettuce is going on tomorrow's subs.
  • Keep the drop-ins looking full by stepping down to smaller pans through the day.
  • Bread and onions left at night go to the grill the next day and have to be used by 2 p.m. After that they're thrown out.
  • Product mix is how many of each kind of sub the store sells. A store selling 80% cold subs uses more lettuce than one selling half and half.
  • Weather counts. Same forecast as last Monday, about the same prep. Cold and rainy, probably less.
