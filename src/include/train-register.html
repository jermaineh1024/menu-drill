The register: keeping it safe

The video's point: paying attention to guests is also what keeps the register safe.

  • Greet every guest as they walk in. Look them in the eye and ask if they need help. Being noticed can discourage a robbery.
  • Cash: lay the guest's bills on the drawer, not in the slots. Make the change, then put the bills away. If there's a question about the change, the bills they handed you are still sitting there.
  • Coins first, then bills, and count the change back as you hand it over.
  • Keep the drawer low. 50s and 100s get checked, then put away out of sight. When bills build up, tell the manager so they can make a drop.
  • Empty the tip jar between rushes.
  • Something looks off? Tell the manager and call the police. Never try to handle it yourself.
  • Wash your hands any time you leave the register. Cash and cards carry germs.

The register: the last impression

The register may be the last contact a guest has with the team, so it decides how they remember the visit.

  • Smile, eye contact, be genuine. Positive without overdoing it. Guests can tell phony.
  • Always ask if they're a rewards member. A guest who misses their points gets upset. The video calls the program Shore Points. It's MyMike's Rewards now, and the points are still called Shore Points.
  • Listen as the wrapper calls the order down. When the line talks, you never have to ask a guest what they ordered.
  • Learn names. An easy way: read the name on the hot sub ticket.
  • Thank every guest, every time, with the smile and the eye contact. The video's cashier offers chips or a drink and closes with "Thanks for stopping in."
