Guests first

Two moments decide what a guest remembers: how they were greeted and how they were thanked. The rest is being yourself and paying attention.

Greet them, in three places

  • Outside: eye contact, a smile, a quick hello. Look for a way to help, like walking a shopping cart back. Pick up any trash you pass.
  • Inside: the same, plus things like opening a door or clearing a table during the rush.
  • At the slicer: greet in the way that fits the person. Recognize your regulars. If someone is new, offer help or a suggestion, and give them room.
  • Be yourself. No scripted "Welcome to Jersey Mike's." They know where they are. Introduce yourself.
  • Learn names. A hot sub ticket has the guest's name on it. Look at their face too, so you know them next time.

While they're here

  • Customers first. When a guest walks in, everyone stops what they're doing and comes to the front line.
  • Banter means friendly small talk. The training calls it "share your life." It has to be natural, and respectful.
  • Keep the talk on the guest. No personal conversations with each other in front of them.
  • 360° vision: know what's happening in the whole store, not just at your station.
  • Peripheral hearing: listen to the whole store while you work, and act on what you hear.
  • Say the order to the next station. Never ask a guest what they had.
  • Urgency: fast and accurate, moving with purpose, and safe.

Thank them

A real thank you to every guest, every single time. The least they should leave thinking is: "I'm going back."

The slicer runs the line

The slicer is the quarterback. They set the pace, watch the whole line, and own anything that goes wrong on it.

  • Experience first, speed second. The slicer watches the front of the line more than the back, and makes sure each order was made right.
  • Controls the pace. Full speed when the line can take it. Slower when the sprinkler or wrapper is backing up.
  • No more than 3 subs at a time. It sets the pace, leaves a moment to check on the team, and means fewer mistakes.
  • Says it down the line: what the sub is and who it's for. The sprinkler tells the wrapper, the wrapper tells the cashier. Nobody has to ask the guest again.
  • Head up. You can't see the whole store looking down.
  • Calls an audible when it's needed. That means changing the play: stop slicing to help sprinkle, or move someone to the spot that's behind. Don't let the grill back up. Send the wrapper to help.
  • Aces in their places: at the start of a shift or a rush, your best people are in their best spots.
  • Takes the blame. On the slicer you aren't only running the slicer. You're running the store.
  • Follows the sub down the line. For example, checks that every club got its bacon.

Seconds count

  • Unwrap everything you'll use before the rush.
  • Pre-tear the hot sub tickets and offset them, so they pull apart fast.
  • Slicer close to the meat case (fewer steps) and close to you (less reach).
  • Pass the sub before you put the last meat away.

The goal, as the training puts it: not to make any one sub faster, but to serve more guests in the same amount of time.

Sprinkler: speak Mike's Way

The sprinkler keeps up with the slicer by sprinkling three subs at once. The trick is to turn every order into Mike's Way, plus or minus something.

  • Speed first, experience second. If the slicer passes 3 subs, sprinkle 3 at a time. Build up to it.
  • Convert the order. "Lettuce, tomatoes, vinegar, oil and spices" is Mike's Way, no onions. One thing to remember, not five.
  • Go topping by topping across all three subs. The hints in the picture tell you which sub is different.
  • Say it back. "That's Mike's Way, no onions, plus mayo and banana peppers." Invite them to order it that way next time. It takes a few visits.
  • Explain Mike's Way loud enough that the next guests in line hear it too.
  • The Sprinkle Dance: two sprinklers whenever possible. One does onions and steps around while the other does lettuce, then comes back for tomatoes, and so on, talking the whole time.

Set up for speed

  • 2 sets of bottles and shakers.
  • Backup extras ready, like the pickle bucket already open.
  • 2 squeegees.
  • Vinegar and oil caps clean and tight.

The sprinkling area, set up

Where everything sits and what has to be ready, from the poster on the meat case door.

You

  • Hands washed, gloves within reach.
  • Gloves on whenever you handle ready-to-eat food.
  • A sanitizer bucket with a towel in the solution.

The board

  • 15 by 20 inches, or up to 30 long, with a towel under it.
  • The trough, the holders, the brown dough cutter and the board scraper.
  • A spatula for the mayo and one for the chopped pepper relish.
  • Don't cut on the sprinkling board.

Stocked

  • Vinegar, oil, oregano and salt full.
  • Backups of onions, lettuce, tomatoes, vinegar and oil ready.
  • Backups of extras prepared with the juices drained.
  • Keep the drop-in fully stocked. Restock on the back counter or in the kitchen.
  • Clean the trough often.

Wrapper: organize, help, expedite

The wrapper is the last person to touch the sub and the first to help everyone else.

  • Speed first, experience second.
  • Organize. Know what every sub is, where it is, and who it's for: front line, digital line, grill, call-ins, catering. Know the names on waiting orders, so pickup is quick.
  • Help the cashier. Ask "chips and a drink?" or "a fresh baked cookie?" yourself. Ask "Are you a Shore Points member?" and point them to enter their phone number at the terminal. Tell the cashier everything you can, so they can ring, smile and give the total.
  • Be the second sprinkler. "Avoid the wrapper lean" means don't stand waiting. Dress the lids with mayo, bacon and cherry pepper relish. Join the Sprinkle Dance. Don't wait to be asked.
  • Expedite. That means getting finished food to the right guest. Every hot sub gets worked into the front line. Use the hot sub ticket to tell the cashier and to find the guest. Don't yell the name. Bring it to them when you can.
  • Last line of defense. Listen as the guest orders, so you know the sub is right. Lift the lid on a club to check for bacon.
  • Never pull a guest ahead in line to ring them up.
  • Spot ways to help: restock the toppings, carry out a big order, check the tables.
  • The last impression is yours: a sincere thank you and a smile.
